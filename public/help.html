The problem

  • Complaints are not need. A 311 encampment report tells you someone complained, not that the people there lack help.
  • Need is distance from help. A block with many reports and no open Pit Stop nearby is a real service gap.
  • Nobody joins the two today. BlockSignal puts live 311 reports next to Pit Stop opening hours, so you can see the gaps at any hour.

How to use it

  1. Pick who's asking. Choose Mayor's Office or Outreach charity. The agent tailors its answers to you.
  2. Pick a time. Set the day and hour to see which Pit Stops are open then. Gaps update to match.
  3. Read the gap map and the Worst Gaps list. Hotter colors mean more reports further from open help.
  4. Click a block to inspect it. See its report counts, the trend, the nearest open Pit Stop and recent crew outcomes.
  5. Ask Outreach Compass. Type a question, tap a suggested question, or press District Briefing for a one-page memo.

Metrics and units

All numbers are per block, never per person. A block (cell) is a grid square of 0.003° latitude × 0.004° longitude, about 330 m × 350 m, or 2 to 3 city blocks.

Gap score in plain words

Many reports + rising + far from open help = high gap. The score runs from 0 to 100 and is relative: 100 is the worst service gap in the current view right now, not an absolute scale.

raw      = ln(1 + count30) × (1 + recent share) × distance_km
distance_km = nearest OPEN Pit Stop in km, clamped to 0.05–2
gapScore = round(raw ÷ max raw × 100)

Worked example: 37 reports in 30 days, 7 of them last week (recent share 7 ÷ 37 ≈ 0.19), nearest open Pit Stop 1.6 km away. raw = ln(38) × (1 + 0.19) × 1.6 ≈ 3.64 × 1.19 × 1.6 ≈ 6.9. If the worst block in view has raw 9.2, this block scores round(6.9 ÷ 9.2 × 100) = 75.

Meet Outreach Compass

Outreach Compass is BlockSignal's AI agent. You ask in plain words. It picks tools, looks up the data, shows each step, and highlights the blocks and Pit Stops it used on the map.

  • Find hotspots
    Blocks with the worst gaps, by district or neighborhood. hotspots
  • Nearest open help
    The closest Pit Stops to a spot, and whether they are open. nearest_open_help
  • Search crew notes
    Find notes by meaning, like "declined services" or "medical need". search_notes
  • Check the trend
    Reports this week compared with last week, by neighborhood. trend
  • Suggest a Pit Stop site
    The block where a new Pit Stop would cover the most unserved reports. suggest_pitstop_site

Under the hood

Mistral

  • Structured outputs turn free-text Pit Stop hours ("Daily 7am to 3pm") into open and close times, and sort crew notes into outcomes.
  • Function calling runs the Outreach Compass agent loop.
  • mistral-embed embeddings power meaning-based search of crew notes.
  • District briefings: a one-page memo per supervisor district.

Elastic

  • geo_point fields on every report and Pit Stop.
  • geotile_grid aggregations find hotspots.
  • geo_distance sorting finds the nearest open help.
  • semantic_text on crew notes for semantic search.
  • ES|QL computes the 7-day trend.
  • Agent Builder hosts Outreach Compass in Kibana, exposed to other clients over MCP.

See how these pieces connect in the interactive architecture diagram.

Guardrails

  • Block level only. Data is grouped into blocks of about 330 m.
  • No individuals, no photos. We never show or analyze people or 311 photos.
  • Services, not enforcement. BlockSignal is for sending help, not for sweeps.
  • Gaps are signals, not ground truth. 311 under-reports in some neighborhoods, so a quiet block is not always a block without need. Check with people on the ground.

Data

All data is public, from DataSF, fetched live and refreshed every hour:

  • SF 311 Cases (encampment reports only, last 30 days)
  • Pit Stop locations and hours

FAQ

How fresh is the data?

It comes live from DataSF and is refreshed every hour. 311 itself lags a little: a report can take some time to appear in the public dataset, and crew notes are added after a visit.

Why does a block with fewer reports rank above a busier one?

Distance to open help. A busy block next to an open Pit Stop has help nearby. A quieter block 2 km from any open Pit Stop can have the bigger gap. Rising reports also add weight.

Is the gap score absolute?

No. It is relative, 0 to 100, where 100 is the worst block in the current view (time and district). Compare blocks within one view, not across days.

What does "open now" use?

The time you pick in "Show Pit Stops open at" (San Francisco time). A Pit Stop is open if that time falls inside its hours, which Mistral reads from DataSF's free-text hours like "Close Time: 5pm to Open Time: 9am". Pit Stops with unknown hours count as not open.

Why Pit Stops as "help"?

They are the only public, geocoded service feed that also publishes opening hours, so we can tell if help is nearby at a given time. On the roadmap: shelters, meal sites and outreach van schedules.

Does it track people?

No. Everything is counted per block of about 330 m × 350 m. There are no individuals, no names and no photos.

Can I use it in Kibana or through MCP?

Yes. The same data lives in Elasticsearch, and Outreach Compass also runs as a Kibana Agent Builder agent whose tools are ES|QL queries, driven by a Mistral connector. Agent Builder's MCP server exposes it to other AI clients. Kibana Maps can show the same layers.

How accurate is the AI?

The tools return real numbers from Elasticsearch (or the app's own copy of the data). Mistral only picks tools, ranks and explains. Every tool call is shown in the panel, so check the numbers it cites before acting.
